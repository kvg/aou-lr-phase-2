# Hit dossier — what to run, and what each run fills in

One page of diagnostics per statistically significant locus, short of the read pileups. Built so the expensive part is
isolated: everything except the regional plot comes out of the harvest cache `felix_08_hit_sweep` already writes.

## The pieces

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `felix/scripts/felix_hit_dossier.py` | the locus rule, the lead match, the checks, the region compaction, the UCSC context |
| `felix/scripts/test_felix_hit_dossier.py` | 56 tests, including that the locus list and the allele split both equal `fig3_real_data`'s |
| `felix/scripts/extract_region.py` | gained `split_windows` / `extract_many` / `--windows`: many windows, one pass |
| `felix/scripts/pull_site_fields.py` | per-sample FORMAT fields at named sites from any indexed VCF, by range-read |
| `felix/scripts/test_pull_site_fields.py` | 27 tests, the query ones against real bcftools on indexed VCFs |
| `notebooks/terra/felix_09_hit_dossier.ipynb` | the Terra export (22 cells, generated by `notebooks/terra/tools/make_felix_09_hit_dossier.py`) |
| `notebooks/terra/tools/check_felix_09_locally.py` | runs the notebook's cell logic against the real tables locally |
| `manuscript_figures/figS_hit_dossier.py` | the renderer: one page per locus, into one multi-page PDF (**uncommitted**: it imports `fig3_real` and `fig3_real_data`, which are deliberately held back with the rest of the Fig 3 figure code) |
| `manuscript_figures/test_figS_hit_dossier.py` | 24 tests on the loader's fallbacks and that a page draws either way |

## Run order on Terra

1. **`00_sync_repo`** — the new and changed modules have to reach
   `$WORKSPACE_BUCKET/felix/scripts/`. Section 1 of the notebook checks for
   `dossier_loci`, `lead_rows`, `annotate_context`, `region_plan`, `compact_region`, `checks`, `verdict`,
   `extract_many`, `read_windows`, `split_windows` and fails with that instruction if the bucket copy is older.
2. **`felix_08_hit_sweep`** — builds `harvest_cache/`. Already done for the 329-run sweep; nothing to redo unless new
   runs have landed.
3. **`felix_09_hit_dossier`** — sections 1-6 take about a minute; section 8 (call rate) needs
   `PRE_PHASING_VCFS` filled in and takes seconds, being 127 index seeks per callset. **Section 7 is the long one**: it streams each
   phenotype's `merged.felix.tsv` (0.5-27 GB) once per arm. 76 phenotypes x 3 arms, so set it going and leave it.
   It is restartable — a locus whose compact files already exist is skipped unless `REDO = True`.
4. Attach the tarball (`$WORKSPACE_BUCKET/scratch/kvg/felix_dossier.tar.gz`) and the files go into the manuscript
   repo's `data/` from here.

So the Terra run is needed for three things — the lead per-ancestry block, the regional extracts and the call rate —
and those are sections 4, 7 and 8.

### The document is now restricted to SVs and repeat expansions (49 of 227 loci)

`render(structural_only=True)`, the default, keeps a locus when its lead is an SV (>= 50 bp) or a repeat-arm hit, **or
when the lead falls inside a UCSC simpleRepeat interval**. The allele string alone is far too narrow: only 8 of the
227 leads are an SV or a repeat-arm hit, while 43 sit in a tandem repeat. A 4 bp TG contraction at a dinucleotide
repeat is a repeat-length change by mechanism even though its alleles read as an ordinary indel, so the repeat context
is part of the rule rather than a refinement of it. The kept set is 49 loci over 46 phenotypes: 2 SV, 6 repeat-arm,
26 indel and 15 SNV leads, the last two classes qualifying through the repeat context.

One consequence had to be handled: **"in a tandem repeat" is now the inclusion criterion, so warning on it says nothing
the reviewer did not choose** -- it fired on 44 of the 49. `checks(repeat_is_context=True)` moves the repeat period to
the detail text and leaves only the pericentromeric band and segmental-duplication conditions driving that status.

### Phenotype calibration: lambda in 0.90-1.20, and why the band is arm-dependent

The chip reads the **lead arm's** `lambda_gc` from the locus row and warns outside `LAMBDA_MIN`-`LAMBDA_MAX`
(0.90-1.20), adding the run's percentile among every run of the same arm when a calibration table is present. It is
the first chip because it is about the scan rather than the variant: a miscalibrated null invalidates every p it
produced, however clean the locus looks.

**The flat band is not equally meaningful across arms.** Measured over the 951-run sweep:

| arm | median lambda | min | max | outside 0.90-1.20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 1.002 | 0.395 | 1.152 | 15 of 951 |
| ru | **0.900** | 0.424 | 1.024 | **476 of 951** |
| ru_biallelic | 0.964 | 0.527 | 1.058 | 206 of 951 |

No main-arm run is inflated above 1.20 at all, so on that arm the upper bound never fires and the chip only ever
reports deflation. The repeat-dosage arm's median sits exactly on the lower bound, so the chip is close to a coin
flip there. The two directions also do not carry the same weight for this document: inflation manufactures false
positives, whereas deflation makes the test conservative, so a hit that reached significance *despite* a deflated
null is not undermined by it. Worth revisiting as per-arm bounds, or as inflation-only, before the final sweep.

### Allelic series: grouped over the repeat tract, 15 loci do grade and 40 do not

**Correction to the first version of this check.** It originally grouped split tests on exact POS and reported 36 warn
/ 191 n/a / **zero pass** over the 227 loci — "not one locus grades". That was an artefact of the grouping. A caller
splits one tandem-repeat tract into records at adjacent coordinates, so exact-POS grouping under-counts: at MS_723
chr3:134,785,621 the +25 bp allele is recorded one base along at 134,785,622 and the check returned n/a instead of
warn. `allelic_series(window=repeat_tract_window(context))` now groups over the tract and the tally is **40 warn / 172
n/a / 15 pass** (on the 49 structural loci: 24 / 10 / 15). Fifteen loci really do show an effect that grades across
the length series; the earlier claim that none did should not be quoted.

`repeat_tract_window` returns period x copies from `felix_dossier_context.tsv`. That is the tract LENGTH, not its
interval, which `locus_context` does not export, so the window is deliberately generous (median 64 bp, p90 619, max
1,464). What keeps the generosity safe is the companion filter: inside a tract only length-changing alleles count,
since the SNVs sharing the window are not alternative lengths of anything. With both, the series size is median 6,
p90 17, max 54, against median 2 under exact-POS grouping. **Exporting the simpleRepeat start/end in felix_09
section 6 would make this exact and is a one-line change on the next VM run.**

`regional_support` asks whether the peak has LD partners, and at a short tandem repeat that question is unanswerable:
recurrent mutation erodes LD with the flanking SNPs, so an empty 100 kb window is expected whatever the truth. The
within-site counterpart survives that objection. felixla splits a merged multiallelic record into one REF/ALT test per
ALT, all sharing the record's POS, so the regional extract already holds the whole allele series; if repeat length
drives risk, the lead's own length neighbours should not be flat.

**40 of the 227 leads carry three or more alleles in their series with the signal confined to a single one**, and 15
grade across the series. The warned set sits nearer the significance margin than the rest. The within-site
multiplicity remains the point the headline p does not carry: a site offering k length contrasts has had k chances,
and that correction is worth applying to the warned set before treating any of them as findings.

Worked example, GI_542.11 (cirrhosis of liver) chr1:26,664,446, dossier page 3: a TG dinucleotide repeat whose record
merges 29 alleles, 13 of them tested. The lead contrast is `AATGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGT` → `AATGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGTGT`
(29 bp → 25 bp, −4 bp) at p 4.18e-8, and **the next best allele at the same site is p 6.2e-2**. The repeat arms do not
settle it either way, because they test different alternatives: `ru` tests a monotone length trend (p 0.059) and
`ru_biallelic` a carrier state that is **69% prevalent** at this repeat (p 0.66). A single-allele effect is invisible to
both, so cross-arm concordance is the wrong check here and the allele series is the right one.

- **The four failures all fail joint-test coherence**: DE_668.1 chr1:105,106,636 (p 2.4e-8), CA_140 chr7:106,687,110
  (3.5e-8), GI_545 chr15:93,448,345 (5.0e-8) and CV_440 chr22:21,651,719 (1.3e-74, which also warns on control F
  +0.447). The joint p at each is unsupported by the per-ancestry statistics it is built from.
- **The positive control got stronger and moved onto the gene.** GE_983.2 (Hemoglobin C / sickle-cell trait) now leads
  at chr11:5,227,002 **inside HBB** with p 3.5e-35 and 43 of 54 case carriers (it was chr11:4,926,224, p 7.3e-17,
  33 carriers). The effect is AFR-only, control allele frequency 0.011 at F +0.027, and it carries **92 supporting
  neighbours within 100 kb, all 92 genome-wide significant**. GE_970 hits the same base pair at p 1.9e-12.
- **138 pages are "incomplete" only because call rate is unassessed** — no page can be complete until section 8 runs

## Open points

- **The case side of Hardy-Weinberg is drawn but not checked.** Panel C shows observed against expected genotypes for
  cases as well as controls, and EM_232.2 for instance has a visible case het deficit, but the `Hardy-Weinberg` check
  reads controls only, which is the convention. Worth deciding whether a case-side departure should raise its own flag.
- **Call rate now has a route, and it is a direct indexed read — no annotate/propagate workflow.** FELIX reads
  `phase_vcfs = flare_lai_prod.gt_vcf`, the ligated phased callset, and phasing fills every genotype: `MissingRate` is
  exactly 0 in all 331,518 cells of the region extracts, so the page reports `n/a` rather than a vacuous pass.
  `felix/scripts/pull_site_fields.py` pulls the genotypes back from any indexed callset at just the lead positions —
  one `bcftools query -R` per VCF, one index seek per lead, nothing localised and nothing merged. htslib range-reads
  `gs://` given `GCS_OAUTH_TOKEN` and `GCS_REQUESTER_PAYS_PROJECT`. Section 8 of `felix_09` runs it against two
  sources, the pre-phasing DeepVariant/GLnexus VCF and the phased `gt_vcf`, and writes `felix_dossier_callrate.tsv`,
  which the Call rate chip reads. **What you must fill in:** `PRE_PHASING_VCFS`, the per-chromosome GLnexus URIs (the
  `GL_INTERVAL_set` table). Allele matching is on (CHROM, POS, REF, ALT), never POS alone — a POS-only match has picked
  the wrong marker in this project before. The second number it yields is the sharper one: the share of the genotypes
  the scan used that phasing **imputed** rather than observed, split by case and control; a gap there is the failure
  mode a call-rate comparison cannot see. 124 of the 127 leads are SNV (78) or indel (46); the 1 SV and 2 repeat leads need
  their own callsets, reachable the same way by pointing `--vcf` at them.
- **The regional-support rule was widened after looking at the render.** "Within 3 orders of magnitude of the lead"
  alone called GE_983.2 a lone peak — at p 7e-17 it demanded neighbours below 7e-14 while the locus carried 77 markers
  at 1e-8 to 1e-13. A neighbour now counts if it is within 3 orders **or** genome-wide significant in its own right,
  whichever is more permissive, and the chip reports how many are. This is the kind of thing only a rendered page
  catches; expect more of it in the polishing pass.
- **Thresholds were set on five 1-Mb windows.** `COHERENCE_MAX = 5.0` sits above the 99th percentile (4.66) of the 67
  rows with `P_het < 1e-3` in the extracts now in `data/`, with CV_446.2's defective lead the only row above it.
  Re-measure once section 4 gives per-ancestry statistics for all 90 loci.
- **Only the top-50 rows of each bundle are screenable at all**, so a hit outside a run's `top_hits.tsv` never reaches
  this document.
